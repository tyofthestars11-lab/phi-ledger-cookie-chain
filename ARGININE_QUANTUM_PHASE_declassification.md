## The finding
Tantalum disulfide's commensurate charge-density-wave state normally survives
only below about -93 °C — a ceiling of 180.15 K, rung 10.7931. Interleaving
layers of the amino acid arginine holds the same quantum phase stable to about
330 K — rung 12.0510 — an extension ratio of 1.8318, rung 1.2579. Arginine fit
the crystal matrix where two other amino acids did not: the biomolecule is not
a passive guest but the structural key to the phase.

## The paradigm shift
Quantum-material design has been a cryogenic discipline: correlated phases as
things that live in the cold and die at room temperature. Here a molecule of
life — an amino acid, the alphabet of proteins — is the stabilizer that brings
a quantum phase across the room-temperature line. Biology is not downstream of
quantum materials; it is a design material for them.
