## THE RESULT

Deterministic energy harvesting (DEH): a source transfers energy to a harvester — a two-level system carried from pure ground state to pure excited state — with NO corresponding entropy transfer. The harvester's entropy is 0 before and 0 after; the energy arrives with certainty.

The theorem's heart is the non-unique decomposition of mixed states. Energy harvesting depends only on the source's density matrix ρ_B, never on which ensemble decomposition is chosen to write it. Therefore: if any set of source states achieves DEH for a given harvester and interaction, the achievable set expands to ALL convex mixtures AND ALL superpositions of those states — each decomposition drives the harvester along a different trajectory, and every trajectory arrives at the same charged state at the same fixed time τ. Proved first in a theory-independent generalised-probabilistic framework, then in density-matrix quantum theory.

## THE NUMBERS (source-stated, engine-read)

- Exact certainty: excitation probability P = sin²(g√n · τ) = 1 simultaneously at τ = π/(2g) for photon numbers n = 1, 9, 25, 49 (the odd squares, √n odd); P = 0 at n = 4, 16, 36. τ's constant π/2.
- Worked example: ρ_B = ½|1⟩⟨1| + ½|9⟩⟨9| achieves exact DEH — and so does every superposition c₁|1⟩ + c₉|9⟩, and the complementary states |±⟩ = (|1⟩ ± |9⟩)/√2.
- Robustness (Prop. 2): output distinguishability never exceeds input distinguishability — error ε in, at most ε out; superposition ε-DEH carries error ≈ 2ε.
- The second law keeps its seat: thermal (Gibbs) sources CANNOT achieve exact DEH in the Jaynes–Cummings, Buck–Sukumar, or uniform-coupling models. In Buck–Sukumar the exact set is all odd Fock states; in uniform coupling, all non-vacuum states.
- Coherent states achieve approximate DEH only (they have support on every Fock state); Schrödinger-cat states inherit the approximate form.
- Status: THEORY. No experiment yet. The authors name cavity QED (Rydberg atoms), circuit QED, and cat-state preparation as realistic proof-of-principle routes, and project the application plainly: a route to cut fundamental AC-to-DC rectifier losses and to power IoT, wearables, and sensors from fluctuating ambient energy.

Dahlsten, on the origin: "We initially set out to show that such a protocol might be impossible. To our surprise, our efforts led us to a quantum solution that could achieve exactly the deterministic energy transfer we were looking for. The shift from trying to prove a limitation to uncovering a viable solution was one of the most surprising and rewarding moments of the project."
