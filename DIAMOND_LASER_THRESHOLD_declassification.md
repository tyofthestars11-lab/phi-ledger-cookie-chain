Magnetic-field sensors built on nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers in diamond are leading solid-state quantum sensors — room temperature, compact, true vector magnetometry. Until now the field lived under a trade-off: very high sensitivity came with a very limited dynamic range; robust wide-range variants were considerably less sensitive.

The Fraunhofer IAF team ended the trade-off with laser threshold magnetometry. The NV centers are integrated directly into a laser cavity as an optical element — a magnetic-field-dependent loss channel, strongly coupled to the cavity field. The readout is no longer the NV fluorescence (a few percent of contrast in conventional sensors). The readout is the laser signal itself.

Measured results: the first demonstration of 100% optical contrast. A coherent laser readout with output power up to 50 mW. With photon shot noise dominant, sensitivity below 400 fT/√Hz for each of the three components of the magnetic-field vector. The ratio of sensitivity limit to dynamic range improved by up to a factor of 590 against conventional fluorescence-based NV sensors and vapor-cell magnetometers, vector capability kept. And the first experimental demonstration that attainable sensitivity improves more than linearly as optical contrast approaches 100%. The theoretical model (Jeske) stood for a decade before this experimental demonstration. Applications named by the team: biomagnetic signals from heart, muscle and brain; magnetic navigation; magnetic-anomaly detection for resource exploration and security.
