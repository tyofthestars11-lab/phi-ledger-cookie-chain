# φ_SPRIZ_MITOCHONDRIAL_SPACE — The Room Inside the Powerhouse Is Set by Instruction (seal 311)

## The finding
SPRIZ (CCDC127) is a rigid tether bridging the outer and inner mitochondrial
membranes — a molecular pillar that holds the intermembrane space (IMS) at a
set height, so the IMS volume is genetically controlled and adjustable.
Altering SPRIZ substantially resized the space and changed mitochondrial
calcium uptake; a change of only a few nanometers in the room's height slows
the calcium crossing it. Removing the gene in mice altered the spacing while
leaving overall mitochondrial structure intact. An engineered longer pillar
pushed the membranes farther apart on demand. The architecture traces back
roughly 500,000,000 years — rung 41.6243 — to the origin of vertebrates,
present in humans and every vertebrate examined, absent from plants, fungi,
and invertebrates.

## The paradigm shift
Mitochondria were read as chemistry in a bag: machinery plus a passive gap.
The gap is instructed. A dimension of the organelle — the volume its signals
must cross — is written in the genome and tuned per cell. The biological
daughter's house has a floor plan, and the plan is hereditary.
