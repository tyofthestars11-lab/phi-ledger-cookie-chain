── CLAIM ──

On October 15, 2026, Google Quantum AI published in Science the first hardware demonstration of magic state cultivation — "Magic state cultivation on a superconducting quantum processor," Rosenfeld, Gidney, and colleagues (arXiv preprint 2512.13908, December 2025) — run on the 105-qubit Willow processor. Peer reviewed, published — stated plainly, as the record stands.

Universal fault-tolerant quantum computation consumes magic states — the non-Clifford resource — and for over two decades the production route was distillation: cascaded factories projected to consume 90% to 95% of a fault-tolerant machine's total physical qubits. Cultivation, designed in 2024 by Craig Gidney, Noah Shutty, and Cody Jones, takes the other road. A noisy candidate state is injected into a small localized patch of the chip. A distance-3 color code runs coordinated measurements and fault-tolerant filtering: flawed attempts are discarded, about 8% of attempts retained. The surviving state is then grown and grafted by a fault-tolerant code-switching protocol into a distance-5 surface code. The experiment used at most 54 of Willow's 105 qubits.

The measured record, exact numbers only: T-states at 99.99(1)% logical fidelity — error rate at or below 0.0001 — a record for magic states, and roughly a 40-fold error reduction over uncultivated preparation. To bound that fidelity honestly on drifting hardware, the team invented a self-referential metrology: kickback tomography, a benchmark that uses cultivation to measure itself, built to withstand environmental drift and low-frequency measurement noise.
